PickList moves items between two independent arrays — `source` and `target` — both exposed as
`model()`s you bind with `[(source)]`/`[(target)]`. Each list keeps its own multi-select (click, or
Enter/Space on the focused row) and its own roving-tabindex keyboard navigation, so arrow keys and
Home/End move focus inside whichever list you're in without touching the other. Four buttons
between the lists move the current selection one way, or move every item at once regardless of
selection — the "move all" buttons ignore what's selected entirely. Double-clicking a single item
transfers just that item immediately, independent of any multi-selection already in place, which is
worth knowing since it bypasses the buttons altogether. Each list also has its own Move Up/Down/
Top/Bottom button column for reordering within that list alone — identical mechanics to OrderList's,
just scoped to whichever list the click came from. `dragdrop` (default `true`) layers native HTML5
drag-and-drop on top of all of it, a mouse/touch-only enhancement, never a replacement: dropping
inside a list's own bounds reorders it, dropping in the other list transfers the item, inserted at
the drop position rather than always appended at the end. Transferring items clears that side's
selection afterward by default; set `keepSelection` to carry the same items' selected state over to
wherever they landed instead. `filterPredicate` (a plain `(item, query) => boolean` function, unset
by default) adds a per-list search box — `showSourceFilter`/`showTargetFilter` (both default
`true`) hide either side's box independently once a predicate is set. `itemDisabled` (a
`(item) => boolean` function) opts individual rows out of every interaction — selection, double-click
transfer, drag — without touching the rest of the list. Beyond the `source`/`target` models
themselves, `moveToTarget`/`moveAllToTarget`/`moveToSource`/`moveAllToSource` fire with exactly the
items that just moved, for a consumer that wants to react to a transfer without diffing the arrays
itself. A shared `<ng-template let-item>` renders each row in both lists.
